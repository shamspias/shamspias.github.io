*You have a cannon, and you have a target sitting somewhere out there. Your job is simple to say and fun to try. Pick an angle for the barrel, pick how much power to use, and fire. The ball leaves the cannon, sails through the air, and either lands on the target or misses. When it misses, you tweak your angle and power and try again. That gentle guessing is the whole game, and it is also a small piece of real science.*

## What you do

You control two things. The angle is how high the barrel points, from flat along the ground to steep toward the sky. The power is how hard the ball is thrown out. A low angle sends the ball fast and close to the ground. A high angle lofts it up in a tall arc. More power sends it farther. Less power keeps it near. To hit the target you find the right pairing of the two. Ready to try it now? Here is the game, [play it](/surprised/#game-projectile) and see.

## What is really happening

Once the ball leaves the cannon, two things happen at the same time. First, it keeps its sideways speed. Nothing is pushing it forward anymore, but nothing much is stopping it either, so it keeps drifting across at a steady pace. Second, gravity is pulling it down, and it pulls harder and harder the longer the ball is in the air. So the ball falls a little in the first moment, more in the next, and even more after that.

Put those two together and the path is not a straight line and not a simple hill. It is a smooth curve that rises, tips over, and comes down. That special curve has a name. It is called a parabola. Think of water arcing out of a garden hose, or a ball you toss to a friend across the yard. Same shape, every time.

## Where it shows up

Once you see this curve, you see it everywhere. A basketball on its way to the hoop. A splash of water from a fountain. A paper plane after your hand lets go. All of them trade a steady sideways drift against a downward pull, and all of them draw that same gentle arc.

## The short version

- The ball keeps its sideways speed after it leaves the cannon.
- Gravity pulls it down faster and faster as it flies.
- Those two together bend the path into a smooth curve, a parabola.
- Aiming is just balancing angle and power until you land on the target.
