*Have you ever sat on a see-saw with someone much bigger than you? If they slide close to the middle, you can lift them, even though you weigh far less. That little trick has a name, and this tiny game lets you play with it.*

## What you do

You drop weights onto a see-saw. Some are heavy, some are light. Your job is to make the plank sit level, so neither side tips down and stays there. You get to choose where each weight lands, near the middle or far out toward the end. Move things around, add one here, shift one there, until the whole thing holds still. That is the entire game, and it is quietly satisfying.

## What is really happening

A see-saw balances when the twisting effort on each side is equal. That twisting effort has a proper name. It is called torque. Here is the surprising part. Torque is not just about weight. It is weight times how far the weight sits from the middle. So a light weight placed far out can balance a heavy weight sitting close in.

Think of pushing a heavy door. Push near the hinge and it barely moves. Push near the handle, far from the hinge, and it swings open easily. Same hand, more twist, only because you moved farther out.

## Where it shows up

This idea is how levers give you strength. A crowbar, a bottle opener, a wheelbarrow, even the claw of a hammer pulling out a nail, they all work this way. You trade distance for power. Sit far from the middle and a small effort does a big job. Once you notice it, you start seeing it everywhere.

Ready to feel it for yourself? [play it](/surprised/#game-balance) and try to make the plank sit flat.

## The short version

- A see-saw balances when the twisting effort, called torque, matches on both sides.
- Torque is weight times distance from the middle, so sitting far out counts for more.
- A light weight placed far can balance a heavy weight placed close.
- This is how levers turn a small push into a big lift.
