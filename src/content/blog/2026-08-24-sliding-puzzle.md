*Have you ever slid little numbered tiles around a small square frame, trying to line them up in order? It looks like a simple toy. But hidden inside it is one of the most useful skills a person can learn: making a plan.*

## What you do

The puzzle is a small grid of numbered tiles with one empty space. You can only slide a tile into that empty space, one tile at a time. There is no lifting and no jumping. To move the tile you want, you first have to move other tiles out of the way. Little by little, you nudge each number toward its home. When they all sit in order, you win. You can [play it](/surprised/#game-fifteen) right now and see for yourself.

## What is really happening

Here is the surprising part. To fix one tile, you often have to move a tile that was already in the right spot. It feels like going backwards. But you are really making room. Grown-ups call this planning. It is the same thing you do when you pack a suitcase, or when you let one car out of a tight parking lot by shuffling the others first. You think a few steps ahead, and you accept a small step back to get a big step forward. That backward step is not a mistake. It is part of the plan.

## Where it shows up

Even stranger: not every shuffle can be solved. If you scramble the tiles at random, only half of the mixes can ever be put in order. The other half are stuck forever, no matter how clever you are. That is not your fault. Some starting points simply have no path to the finish. Knowing when a goal is reachable, and when it is not, is a big part of smart thinking too.

## The short version

- You slide one tile at a time into the empty space to sort them.
- Good moves sometimes mean going backwards first to make room.
- Only half of all random shuffles can be solved at all.
