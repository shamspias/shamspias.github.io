*Try this. Look at a word printed in coloured ink, then say the ink colour out loud and ignore what the word actually spells. Sounds easy, right? Now imagine the word RED printed in blue ink. Your mouth wants to say red, but the answer is blue. That little tug of war is the whole game, and it has a name.*

## What you do

In Colour Trick you see colour words on the screen. Each word is painted in some ink colour. Your only job is to name the ink colour and ignore the word. If the word GREEN is written in orange, the correct answer is orange. Simple to explain, tricky to do.

## What is really happening

Reading is a habit you have practised thousands of times. By now it is automatic, like tying your shoes without looking. When you see a word, your brain reads it whether you want to or not. So when the word and the ink disagree, two answers race to your mouth at once. The reading answer is fast and pushy. The colour answer has to shove it aside. That extra effort is why you slow down and sometimes giggle at your own mistakes. This clash between attention and habit is called the Stroop effect, named after the researcher who first showed it off.

## Where it shows up

The same tug of war appears all over daily life. It is why texting while walking is a bad idea, and why a familiar route can trick you into driving home when you meant to go somewhere new. Your automatic habits are usually helpful, but they do not switch off just because the situation changed. The trick is not a sign that anything is wrong. It just shows how strong a good habit can be.
