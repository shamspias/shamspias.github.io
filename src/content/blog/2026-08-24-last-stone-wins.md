*Imagine a small pile of stones on a table. You and a computer take turns picking some up. Whoever grabs the very last stone wins. It sounds like pure luck, but it is not. There is a hidden rule, and once you know it, you can win almost every time.*

## What you do

You start with one or more little piles of stones. On your turn, you pick one pile and take as many stones as you like from it, at least one. Then the computer does the same. Back and forth you go, and the piles shrink. The person who takes the final stone is the winner. That is the whole game. It is simple to learn and tricky to master. [play it](/surprised/#game-nim) and see how you do against a perfect player.

## What is really happening

Here is the surprise. This game is completely solved. That means clever people worked out a rule that always tells you the best move. The rule is a special way of combining the pile sizes. When you combine them and get a plain, balanced result, you are in a losing spot if the other player is perfect. When the result is unbalanced, you can make a move that flips it. Then you keep flipping it every turn until you take the last stone. The computer in the game knows this rule by heart, so it never slips. Your job is to feel out the pattern by playing.

## Where it shows up

This little puzzle is your first taste of game theory, the maths of winning strategies. Grown-ups use the same kind of thinking in bigger places. Shops decide prices while guessing what rivals will do. Sports teams plan moves while reading the other side. Even animals sharing food fall into these patterns. The stones are just a friendly way in. Once you spot that a game can be solved, you start looking for the hidden rule in everything.

## The short version

- Take stones in turns, and the one who grabs the last stone wins.
- The game is solved, so a counting rule always shows the best move.
- The computer never makes a mistake, so watch and learn its pattern.
- This is your first step into game theory, the maths of winning.
