*Fractions can sound scary, but a pizza makes them friendly. If you can share a pizza with friends, you already understand fractions. This little game turns that everyday idea into something you can see, and once you see it, it clicks.*

## What you do

You look at a pizza on the screen. Some slices are shaded and some are not. Your job is to pick the fraction that matches the shaded part. That is the whole game. Look, count, and choose. When you are ready, [play it](/surprised/#game-fractions) and try a few rounds.

## What is really happening

A fraction is just two numbers with a line between them. The bottom number tells you how many equal pieces the whole pizza was cut into. The top number tells you how many of those pieces are shaded. So three quarters means the pizza was cut into 4 equal slices, and 3 of them are shaded. Easy.

Here is the fun twist. Different fractions can name the same amount. If you shade 2 slices out of 4, that looks exactly like shading 1 slice out of 2. Two quarters and one half cover the very same part of the pizza. When we write a fraction with the smallest possible numbers, like turning two quarters into one half, we call that reducing to lowest terms. It is the tidiest way to say the same thing.

## Where it shows up

Fractions are everywhere once you notice them. Half a glass of juice. A quarter past three on a clock. Splitting a chocolate bar so everyone gets an equal share. Cutting a cake into 8 pieces and eating 3 of them. Every time you share something fairly, you are using fractions, even if nobody says the word.

## The short version

- A fraction means some equal pieces out of a whole, like 3 slices out of 4.
- The bottom number is how many equal pieces, and the top number is how many are shaded.
- Different fractions can name the same amount, so two quarters is one half.
- Seeing it as a shaded pizza makes the idea click.
