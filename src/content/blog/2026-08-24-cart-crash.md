*Have you ever watched a small toy car smack into a big one and bounce straight back, while the big one barely budges? That is exactly what this little game lets you try. You get two carts, and you decide how heavy or how light each one is.*

## What you do
You pick two carts. One can be heavy, like a shopping cart loaded with water bottles. The other can be light, like an empty basket. You send them straight at each other and let them crash head-on. Then you watch what happens to their speeds. You can [play it](/surprised/#game-collide) and test as many weights as you like. Watch closely, because the real surprise is in the speeds right after they touch.

## What is really happening
There is a hidden number that stays the same all the way through the crash. Grown-ups call it momentum. It is really just weight times speed, added up for both carts together. Before the crash and after the crash, that total is exactly the same. Nothing sneaks in, and nothing leaks out. Momentum is a bit like money in a locked box. You can move it between the two carts, but the total amount can never grow or vanish.

Because of this rule, a heavy cart barely changes its speed when a light cart hits it. The light cart, on the other hand, bounces back fast. Think of a marble thrown hard at a bowling ball. The marble zips backward. The bowling ball hardly seems to care.

## Where it shows up
Once you notice this, you see it everywhere. A big truck and a small car in a bump. A bowling ball rolling into the pins. Even in a pool, when you push off a heavy wall and shoot backward, because the wall barely moves. Rockets use it too. They push hot gas out one way and get pushed the other way. It is the same rule every single time.

## The short version
- Momentum is weight times speed, added up for both carts.
- The total momentum stays the same before and after the crash.
- A heavy cart barely changes speed, and a light one bounces back fast.
