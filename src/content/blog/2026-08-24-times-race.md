*Have you ever tried to add up prices at a shop while someone waits behind you? If the small sums come quickly, your mind stays calm and free. That is what Times Race is about. It is a tiny game where you answer times table questions, one after another, until the clock runs out.*

## What you do

The game shows you a question like 6 times 7. You type the answer and the next one appears. You keep going for as long as the timer allows. There is no punishment for a slow round and no trick to it. You simply answer as many as you can, then try to beat your own score next time. Ready to try? [play it](/surprised/#game-times-table) and see how many you reach.

## What is really happening

Here is the real idea. When you know that 6 times 7 is 42 without stopping to work it out, your brain does not have to spend effort on it. That freed effort can go to the harder parts of a problem instead. Think of tying your shoelaces. The first week it takes all your attention. Soon your fingers just do it, and you think about where you are walking.

Small multiplications work the same way. Fast, automatic recall of them clears space in your head for bigger maths, like long division, fractions, or working out a good deal in a shop.

## Where it shows up

This quiet skill turns up everywhere. Splitting a pizza between friends, doubling a recipe, counting how many days until a birthday, checking your change. None of these feel like a maths test, yet each one leans on quick multiplying. The more automatic your recall, the less these moments slow you down.

Like any skill, recall grows with quick repeated practice. A few short bursts beat one long, tiring session. That is exactly what the timer is for. It nudges you to answer fast, and fast answers today become effortless answers tomorrow.

## The short version

- Times Race asks you to answer times table questions before the clock runs out.
- Quick, automatic recall of small multiplications frees your brain for harder maths.
- Like any skill, it grows with short, repeated bursts of practice.
