*Have you ever guessed a secret and used little hints to get closer and closer? That is the whole idea behind a small game called Secret Colours. You pick four colours, watch the hints appear, and let them guide your next try. It looks like simple play, but you are really doing something clever.*

## What you do

There is a hidden row of four colours that you cannot see. Your job is to guess it. After each guess, the game gives you clues. One kind of clue means a colour is right and sitting in the right spot. Another kind means a colour is right but sitting in the wrong spot. You do not get told which colour, only how many. So you read the clues, think, and guess again. Ready to try it? You can [play it](/surprised/#game-mastermind) in your browser.

## What is really happening

Every guess is a tiny experiment. Before you guess, many colour rows are still possible. After you read the clues, some of those rows can no longer be true, so you cross them out in your head. A smart guess is one that removes the most wrong answers at once. Think of the game "Guess Who", where a question like "Do they wear glasses?" flips down a whole group of faces at the same time. Good players do not guess wildly. They pick guesses that teach them the most, even when a guess cannot possibly be the final answer.

## Where it shows up

This kind of thinking has a grown up name: deduction. It is the same reasoning a detective uses to narrow a list of suspects, and the same way a scientist tests an idea to see what it rules out. Doctors do it too, checking one thing at a time until the answer is clear. So while you play with colours, you are practising a real thinking skill.

## The short version

- You guess a hidden code and read clues about how close you are.
- Each guess is an experiment that rules out wrong answers.
- The best guesses are the ones that teach you the most.
- This clever thinking is called deduction, used by detectives and scientists.
